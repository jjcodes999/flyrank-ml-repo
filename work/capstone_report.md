## 0. Abstract

FlyRank publishes content into its clients' websites and then has to decide, from search data,
which of thousands of pages a person should fix first; this case study takes one version of that
problem: pages that Google shows thousands of times but that are clicked far less often than
other pages at the same position. Using one month of the pseudonymized FlyRank internship
warehouse (March 2026; 75,817 pages from 37 client websites), I estimated each page's click gap
from 1–15 March and checked whether its click-through rate (CTR) was still below typical for its
position in 16–31 March. A transparent rule (top-20 position, at least 1,000 impressions,
below-typical CTR, ranked by click gap) was compared with logistic regression and a random
forest on five client-grouped folds, so every score comes from websites the method was not
trained on. The rule's top 20 pages per fold were still under-performing later in 93% of cases
against a 35% base rate; the random forest was slightly more precise at the very top (98%) but
not deeper in the list, and its top 20 covered about half the click gap (362 vs 755 estimated
missed clicks). For FlyRank's "which page first?" decision, the output is a decision-support
review queue with reason codes, human checks and no-go rules, ordered by the simple rule rather
than a model; it does not show that editing a page will raise its clicks.

## 1. Problem framing

- **Decision supported:** which pages a person should review first for a click-through problem
  (the title and description shown in Google), out of tens of thousands.
- **Unit of analysis:** one page (one `content_hash_id` within one client).
- **Output:** a ranked review queue. Each flagged page carries one action, reason codes and a
  confidence label.
- **Human action:** run the data and tracking checks first, then review 20–50 pages per cycle
  from the top of the queue. A person decides what, if anything, to change.
- **Cost of a wrong call:** a false alarm wastes about 20 minutes of an editor's review time; a
  miss leaves a click gap unreviewed for another cycle. With 8,710 flagged pages (roughly 2,900
  hours to review them all), ranking the top correctly matters more than covering everything.
- **Why data/ML helps:** CTR depends heavily on position, so "low CTR" alone is misleading.
  Data lets each page be compared with pages at a similar position and weighted by volume. ML
  was tested as a way to rank better than a fixed rule — FlyRank's "beat the rule" question.
  On unseen websites it did not clearly beat the rule, which is itself the finding.

## 3. Baseline

**Rule:** a page is flagged if, in 1–15 March, its average position was ≤20, it had ≥1,000
impressions, and its CTR was below the median CTR of its position band. Flagged pages (8,710)
are ranked by click gap; all others score 0. It was frozen before any model was trained.

**Why it's fair:** it uses the same five first-half inputs as the models, it was written before
modelling, and it is scored on exactly the same client-grouped folds and metric. Its two
signals were tested first (`w04_baseline_score`): below-typical pages stayed below 54–57% of the
time vs 20–42% for above-typical pages, and persistence rose with volume only above 1,000
impressions (51% → 60%, 70%, 74%).

**Numbers (mean of 5 client-grouped folds):** P@10 0.94, P@20 0.93, P@50 0.924, P@100 0.898,
AUC 0.566 (low by design: every unflagged page scores 0). Base rate 0.351.

## 4. Model / analysis

- **Methods:** logistic regression (log-transformed impressions and signed-log click gap, all
  features standardised) for one readable weight per feature; random forest (200 trees,
  `min_samples_leaf=20`, `random_state=42`) to capture feature combinations. A single small
  decision tree was rejected: too few distinct scores, so the top of the list becomes a tie.
- **Why it fits the lane:** Lane 4 asks for an expected-CTR-by-position gap and ranked scoring;
  both models rank pages by the probability that the gap persists.
- **Features (all from 1–15 March):** `impressions_h1`, `ctr_h1`, `position_h1`
  (impressions-weighted), `days_visible_h1`, `missed_clicks_h1` = impressions × (band median CTR
  − page CTR) ÷ 100.
- **Left out on purpose:** see §2 (second-half numbers, GA4, `gsc_sum_position`, IDs, product
  flags).
- **Target (proxy):** `still_under = 1` if the page's CTR in 16–31 March is below the median CTR
  of its position band in that same period (bands 0–3, 3–5, 5–7, 7–10, 10–15, 15–20, 20–30,
  30–50, 50+).

## 5. Evaluation

**Split:** `GroupKFold(5)` grouped by client — every page is scored by a model that never saw
its website, because pages from one site share templates, brand searches and tracking. Folds
test 1 / 3 / 11 / 11 / 11 clients (fold 1 is a single client with 17,425 pages); fold base rates
0.44 / 0.50 / 0.26 / 0.32 / 0.24, so methods are compared inside each fold.

| Method | P@10 | P@20 | P@50 | P@100 | AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random pick (base rate) | 0.35 | 0.35 | 0.35 | 0.35 | 0.50 |
| **Rule (baseline)** | **0.94** | **0.93** | **0.924** | **0.898** | 0.566 |
| Logistic regression | 0.94 | 0.92 | 0.896 | 0.876 | 0.809 |
| Random forest | 0.98 | 0.98 | 0.920 | 0.884 | 0.805 |

- Random forest vs rule at P@20, fold by fold: 3 wins, 1 tie, 1 loss — not a consistent edge.
- **Value of the top 20** (median impressions / click gap covered): rule 21,342 / 755; LR
  14,397 / 671; RF 9,117 / 362. The forest picks smaller, "surer" pages.
- **Split sensitivity:** with a random split (30–34 of 37 clients on both sides), LR appeared
  to beat the rule at P@50 / P@100 (0.948 / 0.926 vs 0.944 / 0.912). Under the client-grouped
  split it does not. Both models lose 0.01–0.02 AUC when websites are kept apart.
- **Leakage audit (RF):** final AUC 0.805; + a planted leak column 1.000 (harness works);
  − `position_h1` 0.799; − `missed_clicks_h1` 0.806 — no single column carries the answer.

**Error analysis:**
- Only 2 of the forest's 100 top-20 picks were false alarms: both near-zero CTR at a top-3
  position (1 click / 8,975 impressions; 4 / 5,710), also flagged by the rule. The data cannot
  say whether they moved position, gained clicks, or had a tracking glitch.
- 5,869 pages that did stay under were ranked in the bottom half; the largest were mild
  (~2 missed clicks each).
- In the rule's queue, 194 pages have an impossible average position below 1 and 1,676 had zero
  clicks — routed to `check_data_quality` and `check_tracking_first` instead of editing.

## 6. Interpretation

- **What the models found:** permutation importance in the forest is highest for `position_h1`
  (0.083), then `missed_clicks_h1` (0.026); LR weights agree (−1.42 and +0.90). The click gap's
  role matches the signal checks. Position's role is partly a property of the label: far down
  the results typical CTR is ≈0 and a page cannot be below 0, so those pages almost always get
  `still_under = 0`. Not a discovery.
- **Negative result:** learned models did not clearly beat a transparent rule on unseen
  websites, and a random split would have suggested they did. The rule ships; the forest
  becomes a confidence label.
- **Side findings:** pooled position-tier CTR (Top 3 0.437%, Page 1 0.330%, Striking 0.336%,
  Page 3–5 0.141%, Deep 0.038%) follows FlyRank's March 2026 report, but pooling hides client
  differences: for the median client CTR drops from 0.368% (Page 1) to 0.245% (Striking), and
  one client supplies 73% of Page 3–5 impressions. Page age shows no CTR drop-off: 43–49% of
  pages are below typical CTR and 10–12% flagged in every age group.

## 7. Recommendation

How a FlyRank editor would use the queue tomorrow:

1. **Clear the checks first:** `check_data_quality` (194 pages, average position < 1 — do not
   edit), then `check_tracking_first` (1,625 pages with 0 clicks — confirm clicks are recorded
   and the page is indexed).
2. **Review 20–50 pages per cycle from the top** with `review_title_and_meta` (6,891 pages). The
   top 20 carry 5% of the queue's click gap at about 81 estimated missed clicks per page; the
   top 100 carry 13%.
3. **Review dominant clients at site level first** (`dominant_client`: two clients hold 72% of
   the queue and 13 of the top 15) — templates, brand searches, search-result layout.
4. **Look at the real search result before editing:** the title/description shown, any answer
   box or ads above the page, intent match.
5. **Leave pages outside the queue alone:** low-CTR pages under 1,000 impressions → monitor;
   low-CTR pages beyond position 20 → a ranking problem, not a snippet problem.
6. **Re-check monthly:** rebuild if the base rate moves by more than 0.10, P@20 falls below
   0.80, the zero-click share rises above 0.25 (now 0.19), or one client exceeds 60% of the top
   50 (now 0.44).

**Never automate:** title/description rewriting or publishing; deleting, merging or redirecting
pages; acting on data-quality or tracking cases before the check; use outside the population;
judging editors or clients by page scores; reporting the click gap as clicks that will be won.

**Confidence and limits:** directional, decision-support only. One month (March 2026); the label
is a persistence proxy, not proof that editing raises clicks (that needs an experiment); the
population is 23% of March pages; two clients dominate; the rule's cutoff and the population
filter make the baseline slightly optimistic; no confidence intervals beyond fold-to-fold
variation.
